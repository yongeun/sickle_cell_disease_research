**Methodologist** — Borderline — the design instinct is correct and the team has demonstrably executed this exact analysis type before, but as written section d) names a design without specifying it: the active comparator is never identified, CKM stage transition is never operationally defined, and immortal time is addressed with the wrong tool ("landmarking"), so on approach alone this currently sits just below my funding line and is fully rescuable within the 500-word ceiling.

### Critical weaknesses identified

- a: The first 20 words are method and dataset, not novelty: "We will conduct a retrospective target-trial-emulation study in the NIH All of Us Research Program to determine whether..." This is the exact sentence shape of every GLP-1 secondary-data LOI I read, and it fires the "crowded space" reflex before the differentiator appears. The differentiator itself is then demoted into an em-dash aside ("— preventing progression, slowing it, or producing regression to a lower stage —") hanging off the vague verb "alters the trajectory."

- b: The gap is asserted only against randomized trials ("The pivotal GLP-1 RA trials (SELECT, FLOW, SUMMIT) enrolled advanced-stage patients and counted cardiovascular and kidney events"). Nowhere does the LOI acknowledge the crowded observational field. A reviewer who knows DASH-CKMH (NCT07188545, GLP-1/SGLT2i and CKM health outcomes in secondary data) or the AHA's 2026 GLP-1-in-CKM funding theme will conclude either that the applicant is unaware of the competition or is hoping the committee is.

- d: The active comparator is never named. Current text: "an active-comparator, new-user target-trial emulation." This is the single most consequential omission in the application. "Active-comparator" without a named comparator drug is an assertion of rigor, not rigor — and because the population is CKM 1-2, the obvious candidates each fail for a different reason that the applicant must be seen to know: SGLT2 inhibitors have their own independent cardiorenal effects (the contrast becomes head-to-head and biases toward null), sulfonylureas cause weight gain (severe channeling), and metformin is usually already on board (not a new-user decision node).

- d: The target trial is invoked but not specified, and immortal-time bias is addressed with the wrong instrument. Current text: "target-trial emulation with propensity-score overlap weighting, landmarking..." There is no eligibility criterion, no washout despite the "new-user" claim, no time zero, no grace period, and no named causal contrast. "Landmarking" is a delayed-entry device; the canonical fix for immortal time in an emulation is aligning time zero with the initiation decision and handling the grace period by cloning with censoring weights. Using the wrong word here reads to a methodologist as vocabulary acquired secondhand.

- d: The outcome — the entire novelty claim of the proposal — is never operationally defined. Current text: "we will apply published CKM-staging algorithms" is all the reader gets. Staging a person once is not the same as detecting a transition. Missing: how often stage is reassessed, whether a single out-of-range value counts as a transition, whether regression and progression are competing or recurrent states, and how death is handled. Progression to stage 4 and death are competing events; a naive Cox model on "progression" with death censored will overstate progression differences between arms with different mortality.

- f: The impact section never names a decision, a decision-maker, or a deliverable. "This study will yield the first real-world estimates of whether early GLP-1 RA treatment bends the CKM trajectory" is self-referential (it describes the study, not the change), "bends the CKM trajectory" is a metaphor where a number belongs, and "either result directly informs CKM care" asserts impact instead of demonstrating it. Nothing here tells a family physician what would be different at next Monday's 9:15 visit.

- h: The one required supporter is another internal medicine physician in the applicant's own department, and the second name is a cardiometabolic researcher in the same group. The heading asks for "collaboration with other CKM ALLIED healthcare providers" — there is no family physician, no nephrologist, no pharmacist, no dietitian, no care manager, and no one outside the PI's division. "will co-lead the analysis as collaborator" also wastes 3 words restating the heading.

- d: "we will apply published CKM-staging algorithms" and the whole outcome-free methods paragraph — the primary outcome is NEVER defined anywhere in a) through i). The novelty claim rests entirely on "CKM stage transition," and nowhere does the LOI say what a transition is, how it is timed, how many measurements are needed, or how it is censored. I cannot score approach on a study with no stated endpoint.

- d: "an active-comparator, new-user target-trial emulation" — no active comparator is named. For stage-1 adults treated for obesity without diabetes there is no plausible active comparator at all, which is the central design problem of this study, and the LOI does not appear to know it.

- b/f: The irreversibility problem is unacknowledged. CKM stage 2 is defined by diagnoses (type 2 diabetes, CKD, hypertension) that never disappear from an EHR problem list, so under most published operationalizations stage 2 cannot regress. That means "promoting regression" is measurable mainly at stage 1, i.e. via adiposity — and demonstrating that a GLP-1 RA lowers BMI is not a finding.

- e: "We anticipate several thousand stage 1-2 GLP-1 RA initiators, with exact counts and statistical power confirmed in Objective 1." Combined with objective (1) "confirm analyzable sample size," the LOI is asking for $20,000 to find out whether the study is possible. "Several thousand" is a guess, and the applicant has standing Workbench access — so the count could have been run before submission and was not.

- b: Timeliness argument contradicts the data. "As the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge and expanding Medicaid coverage bring these drugs to millions of adults with obesity" — All of Us EHR follow-up runs through roughly 2023. The proposal's urgency rests on 2026 policy that the dataset cannot possibly observe, and it never says what period the data cover.

- f: "This study will yield the first real-world estimates of whether early GLP-1 RA treatment bends the CKM trajectory." I can defeat "first" in one search: GLP-1 RA prescription-pattern and CKM analyses in All of Us exist (medRxiv 2026), target-trial emulations of GLP-1 RAs on CV/kidney outcomes in obesity exist (2025), and DASH-CKMH (NCT07188545) is actively studying GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i effects on CKM health outcomes. "Bends the CKM trajectory" is a metaphor standing in for a result.

- h: Non-responsive to the question asked. The heading is "Collaboration with other CKM allied healthcare providers (identify at least one person who supports your project)." The answer names two physicians in the applicant's own institution and division, neither described as an allied CKM provider, and never says anyone supports the project. "Dr. Marie S. Thearle provides additional cardiometabolic content expertise" is consultant filler.

- i: "Because all data are de-identified and already available through the Workbench, the project requires no participant recruitment and no external data-use agreements." The no-DUA claim is likely factually wrong: All of Us Controlled Tier EHR access requires a Data Use and Registration Agreement plus an institutional agreement and per-researcher credentialing.
