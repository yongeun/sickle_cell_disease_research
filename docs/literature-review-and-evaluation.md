# Can GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Prevent the Development or Progression of CKM Syndrome?

**Literature scan and research-question evaluation for a secondary analysis using the *All of Us* Research Program**

_Prepared: July 2026 · Intended funding target: Bayer/AAFP REACH Awards (CKM research concepts, up to $20,000)_

---

## 1. Background: the research question in context

**Cardiovascular–Kidney–Metabolic (CKM) syndrome** is the American Heart Association's 2023 construct describing the interlocking pathophysiology of excess/dysfunctional adiposity, type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and cardiovascular disease (CVD). It is staged 0–4:

| Stage | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| 0 | No CKM risk factors |
| 1 | Excess/dysfunctional adipose tissue (overweight/obesity, prediabetes) |
| 2 | Metabolic risk factors (T2D, hypertension, dyslipidemia) and/or CKD |
| 3 | Subclinical CVD, or high predicted CVD risk / very-high-risk CKD |
| 4 | Clinical CVD in the setting of CKM |

The 2026 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN CKM guideline explicitly frames the clinical goal as **preventing stage progression and promoting stage regression** — not just treating end-stage events. That reframing is what makes "can a drug move people *down* the staging ladder, or keep them from moving up it?" a live, guideline-relevant question.

**GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs)** — semaglutide, liraglutide, and the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist tirzepatide — sit at the mechanistic center of CKM because a single drug class simultaneously targets weight, glycemia, blood pressure, kidney function, and atherosclerotic risk.

## 2. What the literature already establishes

**Strong randomized evidence at the *late* stages (CKM 3–4):**
- **SELECT** — semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced MACE in adults with obesity and established CVD *without* diabetes (first dedicated CV outcomes trial in obesity).
- **FLOW** — semaglutide slowed kidney disease progression and reduced kidney/CV events in T2D + CKD.
- **SUMMIT / SURMOUNT** and pooled analyses — tirzepatide improves weight, glycemia, and surrogate renal/CV endpoints; a strong multidimensional renal signal, and benefit in HFpEF + obesity.
- 2025 comparative and trial-emulation analyses (incl. AHA Scientific Sessions 2025) show semaglutide and tirzepatide confer broadly similar CV benefit in high-risk obesity/diabetes populations.

**Narrative/mechanistic reviews** (JACC: Advances 2025; Biomedicines 2025; a 2025 JACC-family review of GLP-1RAs across CKM stages) now explicitly position GLP-1RAs — often paired with SGLT2 inhibitors — as CKM disease-modifying therapy, and call for **earlier and combination use**.

**Real-world / *All of Us*-specific work already published or underway:**
- CKM stages 0–4 have been **operationalized in *All of Us*** using EHR, labs, and physical measurements (one study staged the cohort and found ~23.5% with advanced CKM; stage distribution roughly 0: 10%, 1: 21%, 2: 46%, 3: 9.5%, 4: 14%). An **ICD-10-CM CKM coding framework** (JMIR Diabetes 2026) and simple **stage-2 risk-stratification algorithms** now exist.
- **GLP-1RA prescription-pattern** and disparity analyses in *All of Us* (medRxiv 2026; earlier JAHA work on inadequate uptake by CV risk and sociodemographic group).
- **Target-trial-emulation** studies of GLP-1RAs on CV/kidney *hard outcomes* in obesity (2025).
- **DASH-CKMH (NCT07188545)** — an active secondary-data study of **sex-based heterogeneity** of GLP-1RAs and SGLT2i on CKM outcomes.
- The AHA has a 2026 funding theme on **"effects of GLP-1 medications among individuals with CKM disease,"** signaling field-level demand.

## 3. The specific gap

Existing work has established, separately: (a) that CKM can be *staged* in *All of Us*; (b) that GLP-1RAs reduce *hard CV/kidney events* mostly in *stage 3–4* trial populations; and (c) that uptake is unequal. What is **not** yet done:

> A longitudinal, stage-transition–focused analysis asking whether **initiating a GLP-1RA slows progression across CKM stages — or promotes regression — particularly at the early, potentially-preventable stages (CKM 1–2)**, in a diverse real-world U.S. population.

Trials enrolled late-stage patients and measured events, not stage movement. The primary-prevention question ("keep a stage-1–2 patient from advancing") is exactly the question a **family physician** faces first, and it is the least-answered by existing RCTs — which is why it fits both the guideline's regression/prevention framing and the AAFP REACH audience.

## 4. Evaluation against the three standards

### Standard 1 — Novelty

**Assessment: Moderate–High, *if* scoped to stage transition / early-stage prevention.**

- ✅ **Novel framing:** CKM-*stage progression and regression* as the outcome (not MACE/ESKD) is new; RCTs did not measure it, and published *All of Us* CKM work is cross-sectional.
- ✅ **Novel population emphasis:** early stages (1–2) and a primary-care lens, where trial evidence is thinnest.
- ⚠️ **Crowded neighborhood:** GLP-1RA + CKM real-world analyses, *All of Us* CKM staging, and GLP-1RA target-trial-emulations already exist; DASH-CKMH is doing sex-heterogeneity. **The proposal must differentiate clearly** — the stage-transition outcome and early-stage prevention focus are the differentiators, not "GLP-1 and CKM" broadly.
- **Verdict:** Novel enough to be fundable at the pilot/REACH scale, provided the aims foreground *stage transitions* and avoid re-deriving known event benefits.

### Standard 2 — Feasibility

**Assessment: High (for a $20k secondary-data pilot); moderate on causal strength.**

- ✅ **Data exist and are proven usable:** *All of Us* has EHR, meds, labs, physical measures, and survey/SDOH data on 400k+ with EHR; CKM staging has already been implemented there, so the hardest engineering step is de-risked.
- ✅ **Exposure ascertainable:** GLP-1RA initiation is captured in medication records; an active-comparator, new-user (target-trial-emulation) design is standard and publishable at this scale.
- ✅ **Right-sized budget/timeline:** Researcher Workbench access is low-cost; analysis is computable within ~12 months by a small team — a good match for a ≤$20,000 award.
- ⚠️ **Real constraints to pre-empt in the design:**
  - *Confounding by indication* — who gets a GLP-1RA differs systematically; requires active comparator, propensity methods, negative controls.
  - *Immortal-time / prevalent-user bias* — enforce new-user design and grace periods.
  - *Adherence ≠ prescription* — fills/refills are imperfect; sensitivity analyses needed.
  - *Follow-up length* — EHR through ~2023 limits observable stage transitions, especially for newer agents; power at early stages (few stage-1 initiators) is the main risk. Pre-specify a power/feasibility count as Aim 0.
  - *No true randomization* — frame as hypothesis-generating association, explicitly.
- **Verdict:** Highly feasible to *execute*; the scientific care is in causal-inference rigor, which is achievable with well-known methods.

### Standard 3 — Interest (to others)

**Assessment: High.**

- ✅ **Peak clinical salience:** CKM is the AHA's flagship cardiometabolic framework with a brand-new (2026) multi-society guideline; GLP-1RAs are the most-discussed drug class in medicine. The intersection is squarely in the field's spotlight.
- ✅ **Perfect audience fit for the funder:** the REACH Awards target **family physicians "uniquely positioned to bridge the gap in CKM care."** An *early-stage prevention* question answered in primary-care–relevant terms speaks directly to that audience.
- ✅ **Equity angle:** *All of Us* is built for diversity; layering *who progresses and who benefits* (race/ethnicity, sex, income, insurance, rurality) onto the question adds broad interest and matches documented GLP-1RA access disparities.
- ✅ **Actionable:** a positive early-stage signal supports earlier GLP-1RA consideration; a null/attenuated signal is also informative for stewardship and cost.
- **Verdict:** Strong general and funder-specific interest.

### Summary scorecard

| Standard | Rating | One-line rationale |
|----------|--------|--------------------|
| **Novelty** | ◐ Moderate–High | New *stage-transition/regression* outcome & early-stage lens in a crowded GLP-1-CKM space — differentiation is essential. |
| **Feasibility** | ● High | Data + CKM staging already proven in *All of Us*; right-sized for a $20k, ~12-month pilot; causal rigor is the work. |
| **Interest** | ● High | Guideline-timely, GLP-1 hype, ideal AAFP/primary-care + equity fit. |

**Overall:** A fundable, competitive pilot **provided the aims are sharpened** to (1) CKM *stage transition/regression* as the primary outcome, (2) an *early-stage (1–2) prevention* emphasis that RCTs have not covered, and (3) an *equity/subgroup* layer that leverages *All of Us*'s design — while explicitly positioning results as hypothesis-generating.
